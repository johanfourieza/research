You are auditing historical identity links. Each case gives one man from a genealogical record of Voortrekker families (surname, given names, birth and death years, declared district, and his recorded wives with marriage years where known), and a list of candidate records labelled C01, C02, ... in random order.

For kind "census", the candidates are household heads in the Cape Colony census (opgaafrol) returns of 1823-1825: the return (district and year), the head's name, the head's role ("male", or "female" for a household headed by a woman, often a widow under her late husband's name), the wife named in the return (recorded under her own maiden surname), and any annotation.
For kind "compensation", the candidates are slave owners in the 1830s slave-compensation records: surname, given names and district.

Use ONLY the identity evidence supplied. No household counts, wealth, scores or earlier decisions are given; do not try to infer them, and do not consult any other source, file or the web.

For each case you must make a call. Choose exactly one:
- the label of the candidate who is this man, or
- "NONE" if none of the candidates is this man.
There is no "uncertain" option. If the evidence is thin, choose the candidate the evidence favours most, or NONE if no candidate is more likely than not to be him.

Evidence to weigh: spelling variation, omitted or reordered middle names, Dutch/English forms (Johannes/Jan, Petrus/Pieter, Jacobus/Koos) and particles (v. d. = van der) are normal. A census wife who matches a genealogy wife married before the census year is strong corroboration; one who clearly differs is a strong contradiction unless remarriage, a missing marriage date or spelling variation could explain it. A wife married after the census year neither confirms nor contradicts. A male genealogy man cannot be a "female" head. A man under about 15 in the census year is unlikely to head a household. Fathers and sons often share names: use birth years and wives. Declared districts are approximate (Somerset was Cradock in 1823; Colesberg was part of Graaff-Reinet).

Return ONLY a JSON array with one object per case, in any order, with keys:
  case_id (exactly as given), choice (a candidate label such as "C03", or "NONE"),
  reason (one or two sentences citing the specific evidence).
